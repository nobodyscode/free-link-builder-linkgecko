# Community replies

Find discussions where the user has something useful to add, then draft replies that people would value even if they never clicked anything.

Read `references/evidence-rules.md` and `references/writing-rules.md` before you begin. If LinkGecko tools are connected, follow `references/linkgecko-connector.md`.

For Reddit, switch to the `reddit-link-builder` skill.

## 1. Get the brief

Read the brand's site, then confirm in one message what it does, who buys it, and who it does not suit. Ask who will post, and what first-hand experience that person has. The reply must be true in their voice.

If the user already has a thread URL, go to step 3.

## 2. Find the threads

Search for buyer language rather than product language. Combine these with the forum's name or a `site:` filter:

- The problem in the words of someone who has it
- "{competitor} alternative" and "switching from {competitor}"
- "how do you {job}" and "what do you use for {job}"
- The category plus a constraint, such as team size, budget or an integration

Keep threads that are open to replies, on topic, and either recent or ranking for the search. List up to ten with the URL, the forum, the date and the question asked.

## 3. Read the thread and the rules

Read the full thread, including the best replies. Then read the forum's rules on self-promotion, using the notes in `references/forum-notes.md`.

If you cannot read the thread, ask the user to paste it. Do not draft a reply to a thread you have not read.

## 4. Decide whether to reply

Choose one verdict and give the reason:

- **Reply and mention:** the asker wants a tool, the product meets their constraints, and the rules allow it.
- **Reply without a mention:** the user can help, but the product is not the answer or the forum bans promotion.
- **Skip:** the thread is closed or off topic, a good answer already says everything, or the user lacks real experience.

Skipping is a good result. A removed reply costs more than a missed thread.

## 5. Draft the reply

Follow these rules for every reply:

- Answer the question that was asked, using a detail from the thread.
- Give the reader something usable: the method, the setting, the number, or the mistake to avoid.
- Keep it to 90 to 180 words, written as prose.
- Mention the brand at most once, near the end, and disclose the connection: "I work on {brand}, so weigh that accordingly."
- Include a link only when the rules allow it and the page directly answers the question.
- Use placeholders for first-hand facts you do not have.

## 6. Deliver

For each thread give the URL, the verdict and reason, the draft, and any rule the user should check. Tell the user to post from their own account after editing the draft into their own words. Do not post for them.
