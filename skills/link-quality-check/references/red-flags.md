# Red flags

One flag rarely decides anything. Look at the pattern and weigh serious flags most.

## Serious

- **No audience:** posts have no comments, shares, newsletter, social following or named community. Confirm with traffic data when the user has it.
- **Topic sprawl:** software next to casino, CBD, loans, essays and travel, with no editorial line connecting them.
- **Links for sale:** a public price list, "dofollow" advertised as a feature, or unlabelled posts that exist to carry a commercial link.
- **Network signs:** near-identical templates, shared authors or shared outbound links across a set of sites.
- **Domain swap:** the current content has nothing to do with the domain's name or earlier history, which can point to a bought expired domain. Check the Wayback Machine when you can.
- **Reputation lease:** a respected site hosting a third-party section unrelated to its main subject, such as coupons or casino reviews.

## Moderate

- "Write for us" pages that accept almost any topic
- Authors with no profile, a stock photo or no other published work
- Posts with many outbound commercial links and little original detail
- Generic text that contains no first-hand experience, data or examples
- Exact-match anchors repeated across posts
- A traffic chart that collapsed recently, when the user has this data
- Ranking tables whose top spots carry a "sponsored" mark

## Good signs

- Named editors and writers with a track record
- A clear audience, such as a newsletter, forum, conference or customer base
- Original data, testing, screenshots or reporting
- Corrections, update notes and a published editorial policy
- Sponsored content that is clearly labelled and separated
- Other trusted sites in the category citing it

## Metrics

Domain Rating and Domain Authority estimate a link profile. Sellers can inflate them, so never let them overrule what you saw on the page. Organic traffic and its trend reveal more, and readers who match the buyer reveal the most.
