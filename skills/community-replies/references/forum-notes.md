# Forum notes

Rules change, so read the forum's current guidelines before you rely on these notes.

## Quora

Questions can rank for years, which makes older threads worth answering. Answers from people with visible credentials tend to read better, so write in the first person and state the credential. Disclose any connection to a product you mention.

## Hacker News

Readers are technical and quick to flag marketing. Comments work when they add technical detail or a first-hand account. Mention your own product only when it is directly relevant, and say that it is yours. Links to your own work belong in a Show HN post that follows its guidelines.

## Stack Exchange

Answers must answer the exact question and stand on their own. The network requires you to disclose affiliation with anything you recommend, and repeated self-links get removed as spam. Treat a recommendation as optional and the solution as the point.

## Indie Hackers and founder communities

Readers respond well to real numbers and honest accounts of what failed. Plain self-promotion gets ignored. Sharing what you learned, with the product named in passing, fits the culture.

## Product Hunt discussions

Launch comments and discussion threads welcome makers. Disclose that you made the product and focus on the problem it solves.

## Niche forums and vendor communities

Read the rules and a few recent threads first. Many ban links for new accounts. Some have a dedicated promotion section, which is where any promotion belongs. On a competitor's community, answer only when the question is genuinely open to other tools.

## GitHub Discussions and issues

Stay on the project's topic. Suggesting your commercial tool in someone's issue tracker is usually unwelcome unless the maintainer asks for options.
