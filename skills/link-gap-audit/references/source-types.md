# Source types

Classify each page so the plan can give it the right action.

## Types

- **Best-of list:** a ranked or curated list on a publisher or vendor site. Check who wrote it and whether the publisher sells the top spot.
- **Comparison page:** "{A} vs {B}" or an alternatives page. Pages run by a rival rarely add you, while independent ones often will when you offer real details.
- **Review site:** G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Trustpilot, Product Hunt and category-specific sites. Profiles are usually open to any vendor.
- **Directory:** a category listing. Keep the ones buyers actually use and skip general web directories.
- **Community thread:** Reddit, Quora, Stack Exchange, Hacker News and niche forums.
- **News or trade press:** articles and roundups in publications your buyers read.
- **Podcast or video:** episode pages, show notes and review videos.
- **Reference:** Wikipedia, Wikidata and industry wikis. These have strict rules on notability and conflicts of interest, so flag them and do not plan promotional edits.
- **Resource page:** a curated list of tools or guides on a school, association or community site.

## Warning signs

Flag a page when you see two or more of these:

- The site covers many unrelated topics with no clear audience
- Posts exist mainly to link out, often with exact-match anchors
- Casino, CBD, loan or essay links sit beside software content
- A public price list for "dofollow" posts, or a "write for us" page that accepts any topic
- A rankings table sold as placements, with "sponsored" spots on top
- Content that does not match the domain's name or history
- Authors with no profile, or stock photo bios

These signs call for a closer look with the `link-quality-check` skill. They do not prove a site is bad.
