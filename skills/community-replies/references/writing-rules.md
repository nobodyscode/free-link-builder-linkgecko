# Writing rules for drafts

Editors, hosts and forum members delete anything that reads like generated marketing. A draft must sound like one knowledgeable person writing to another.

## Rules

- Open with the substance. Skip the greeting line in forum replies and skip the flattery everywhere.
- Say one specific thing the reader can use: a number, a setting, a method, or what went wrong.
- Use plain words and full sentences. Keep most sentences under 25 words.
- Write about the reader's situation before the brand's.
- State the brand's limits when they matter. One honest limit does more for trust than three adjectives.
- Use only facts from the user, their site, or a public source you can name. Put a bracketed placeholder where a real detail is needed.

## Words and patterns to remove

- Em dashes and en dashes used as punctuation.
- Openers such as "Great question", "Hope you're well", "I came across your article", "As someone who", "Here's the thing".
- Reveals such as "what most people miss", "the truth is", "the answer might surprise you".
- Contrast frames such as "it's not just X, it's Y".
- Rhetorical questions, including "Sound familiar?".
- Marketing words: seamless, robust, leverage, game-changing, cutting-edge, unlock, elevate, best-in-class, all-in-one.
- Sign-offs such as "Hope this helps" and calls to action such as "Check it out".
- Headings, bold text and bullet lists in forum replies, unless the answer is truly a short list.

## Final check

Read each draft once as the recipient. Remove any sentence about the brand that would still make sense with another company's name in it.
