# Pitch playbook

Each pitch earns its reply by saving the recipient work or giving their readers something they would miss.

## Best-of list or comparison

**What they want:** a more useful list and no extra work.

1. Name the buyer the list currently underserves, or the criterion it uses.
2. Give one concrete reason the product fits, and include a limit.
3. Offer what makes testing easy: a free account, a short demo clip or real pricing.
4. Provide a two-sentence description they could paste in.

Keep it under 130 words. Admit that you work on the product in the first or second sentence.

## Unlinked mention

**What they want:** accurate pages that help readers, with no fuss.

1. Thank them in plain words and name the article.
2. Give the exact sentence where the brand appears.
3. Give the exact URL to link, and explain in a few words how it helps their reader.
4. Add any correction if a detail has gone out of date.

Keep it under 70 words. Make no other request.

## Journalist or expert request

**What they want:** a usable quote before the deadline.

1. Start with the answer to what they asked.
2. Add one number, example or first-hand detail that makes it quotable.
3. Give the source's name, title and one line of credentials.
4. Say how to reach them for follow-up.

Keep it under 150 words. Meet the request's criteria exactly, and skip it when the source does not qualify. Answer as soon as you can, since many requests close within days.

## Podcast guest

**What they want:** an episode their audience finishes.

1. Reference a real episode or theme of the show. Ask the user to listen if you cannot verify one.
2. Offer two or three specific topics this audience would care about.
3. Give one result or story that proves the guest has done the work.
4. Link to a past interview if one exists.

Keep it under 140 words. Pitch the guest's experience and leave the product in the background.

## Resource page

**What they want:** a page their community keeps using.

1. Name the section where the resource fits.
2. Say what the resource does for that audience.
3. State that it is free, if it is. Paid tools rarely belong on these pages, so say so to the user first.

Keep it under 90 words. Pitch a free tool, guide or template over the homepage.

## Outdated detail

**What they want:** to avoid being wrong in public.

1. Quote the outdated line.
2. Give the correct detail and its source.
3. Disclose that you work for the brand, if you do.

Keep it under 80 words. Ask for the correction and nothing else.
