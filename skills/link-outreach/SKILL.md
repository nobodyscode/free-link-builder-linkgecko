# Link outreach

Write a short pitch for one specific person about one specific page. The user reviews and sends it.

Read `references/evidence-rules.md` and `references/writing-rules.md` before you draft. If LinkGecko tools are connected, follow `references/linkgecko-connector.md`.

## 1. Gather the inputs

You need the target URL or request text, plus the brand's domain. Read the brand's site for what it does, who it serves and what it costs.

Ask the user for proof you cannot find: customer counts, results, a founder's background, or data they could share. One real detail decides whether a pitch gets a reply, so ask for it directly.

## 2. Read the target

Read the whole page or request, then note:

- Who wrote it and who it serves
- What the page says about the category, and what it leaves out
- Which competitors it covers and how it judges them
- Whether the brand meets the page's own criteria

**Stop here if the brand does not fit.** Tell the user why and suggest a better target. A pitch to a list that the product does not belong on burns the contact.

If you cannot read the page, say so and ask the user to paste it. Do not write a personal pitch from a search snippet.

## 3. Choose the pitch type

Open `references/pitch-playbook.md` and use the section that matches: best-of list, unlinked mention, journalist request, podcast guest, resource page or outdated detail.

## 4. Find the contact route

Use contact details the person has published: an author page, a site contact form, a press address or a public profile.

Do not guess email patterns or dig personal addresses out of data sites. If you find no route, say so and name where the user could look next.

## 5. Write the draft

Follow the length and structure in the playbook. Deliver:

- Two subject lines
- The message, under 130 words unless the playbook says otherwise
- Placeholders for any fact you lack, such as `[your real number]`
- One follow-up of under 50 words, to send after 5 to 7 days if there is no reply

Stop after one follow-up. A second nudge rarely helps and costs goodwill.

## 6. Check the offer

Warn the user when a reply asks for payment. Google's spam policies treat paid links that pass ranking credit as link spam, and it asks publishers to mark them with `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`. A paid placement may still be worth buying for its readers, as long as the link is marked and the post is labelled.

Run `link-quality-check` first when the site looks doubtful.

## 7. Deliver

Show the draft in the conversation and include:

- The contact route and where you found it
- The one detail that makes this pitch specific to this person
- Any claim the user must verify before sending

Do not send the message. For more than five targets, draft them one at a time and give each its own specific detail. Identical pitches with swapped names are spam.
